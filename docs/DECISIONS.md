# Decision log

Key modeling and engineering choices, with the reasoning behind each. This doubles as pitch-deck material.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Alternatives rejected |
|---|------|----------|-----|-----------------------|
| D1 | 2026-10-03 | Run the same engine on both sites, then let the results pick the pitch site | The brief requires us to justify our site choice. Doing it quantitatively is our innovation hook | Picking a site by intuition |
| D2 | 2026-10-03 | Split the optimization into a design MILP (peak and annual), then an 8760-hour LP dispatch, then a rule-based dispatch for Monte Carlo | A combined 8760 × binary MILP takes minutes to hours to solve. Splitting it keeps every scenario solving in seconds, so we can precompute many what-ifs | A single monolithic MILP |
| D3 | 2026-10-03 | No basemap tile server. The map draws only bundled GeoJSON (streets, water, footprints) | The demo must work with wifi off (Execution rubric) | MapLibre demo tiles, Protomaps |
| D4 | 2026-10-03 | Precompute structural scenarios. Recompute carbon price and grid decarbonization in the browser | These two are linear in hourly arrays we already ship, so the sliders stay instant with no backend | A live Python backend |
| D5 | 2026-10-03 | Streets come from NYC Street Centerline (CSCL) for Site 1 and Census TIGER roads for Site 2, not from OSM | Every Overpass endpoint refused or hung connections from our build machine. CSCL is the city's own authoritative centerline (the source behind LION), so it's a better basis for routing pipes in public rights-of-way | OSMnx/Overpass |
| D6 | 2026-10-03 | Equity attributes come from CDC SVI 2022 (which embeds ACS 2018–22 estimates) and DOE LEAD 2022. We don't call the Census API | The Census API began requiring a key and returned an HTML page with HTTP 200. SVI and LEAD carry the household, poverty, housing-burden, income and energy-burden fields we need. `fetch()` now validates payloads, so error pages can never be cached as data | Census ACS API |
| D7 | 2026-10-03 | Derive 111 8th Ave IT load from the building's own LL84 electricity (57.7 MW avg) minus an LL84-derived office baseline, divided by PUE. Result: 24–37 MW IT, central value 30 | No public IT figure exists. A data-derived range from the building's own mandatory disclosure is defensible when judges probe it. A guessed number is not | Unsourced 5–30 MW guess |
| D8 | 2026-10-03 | Hourly price is NYISO DAM zonal LBMP, with a labeled proxy fallback (EIA-930 load shape scaled to the 2023 SOM published zonal average) | mis.nyiso.com was intermittently unreachable. The real 2023 data was fetched and cached, and the proxy remains a tested fallback that is labeled in the UI | — |
| D9 | 2026-10-04 | Site 1 offtakers are only buildings with measured LL84 consumption, aggregated per LL84 property (campus) | Measured demand beats synthetic demand when judges probe numbers, and LL84 covers every building above 25k ft² (the economically relevant ones) | Synthetic demand for every footprint |
| D10 | 2026-10-04 | The matcher scores the brief's five dimensions plus proximity and equity, each 0–1 and shown separately. Continuity uses an occupant-type rubric | Explainability: each rank comes with per-dimension bars and a one-line rationale | A single opaque score |
| D11 | 2026-10-04 | Screening rules are explicit and sourced: named exclusions (Penn South CHP, NYU CoGen, the DC itself), a fossil-intensity cap, and a **cogeneration signature** (grid electricity <30% of the peer median while fossil fuel >2× the peer median). Every excluded building is listed with its reason | LL84 gas at cogeneration sites is power generation, not heat demand. PENN 1 was 27 MW of phantom demand that captured most of the supply before this rule existed | Trusting LL84 fuel as heat demand |
| D12 | 2026-10-04 | Network supply temperature follows outdoor reset (65 °C floor, DHW-safe, rising to 80 °C on the design day). COP is demand-weighted over the hourly COP (avg 3.28) | 4GDH practice. A fixed 75 °C understated COP and made gas customers look unviable | Fixed supply temperature |
| D13 | 2026-10-04 | Value streams: customers' avoided fuel, minus HP electricity, **plus avoided DC cooling energy** (the HP evaporator does the DC's cooling work), plus LL97 penalties avoided (×0.75 capture, 0 for NYCHA's separate pathway). The equity shadow price ($/MWh × SVI) shapes the design but is **excluded from cash NPV** | The brief asks for measurable value to the DC. Keeping the equity value as an explicit design lever, not as cash, keeps the financials honest | Counting equity value as revenue |
| D14 | 2026-10-04 | MILP: fixed-charge single-commodity flow on the real CSCL street graph (427 nodes, 670 edges, 62 candidates, ~730 binaries). Pipe cost uses a linear fit of the DN table, and the final design is re-costed by DN. Tight big-M (HP max) plus a node-incidence valid inequality. HiGHS solves to a 0.5% gap in about 70 s | Tractable and exact enough. The gap is reported in the UI | Discrete DN binaries per edge (5× the binaries) |
| D15 | 2026-10-04 | Validate the demand model against metered NYCHA steam bills (Fulton 2023): monthly R² = 0.975, annual within 3% | Turns "we modeled demand" into "we checked it against real bills". Also yields the real steam price ($21.3/MMBtu), which replaced an estimate of $35 | — |
